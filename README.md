# CS-250-Portfolio
CS 250 Software Development Lifecycle portfolio

Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250 at Southern New Hampshire University. The project
involved developing software for SNHU Travel using Agile and Scrum practices. Throughout the course, I explored different
Scrum roles, the importance of communication between development teams, and how Agile methods can help teams respond to
changing requirements.

Interpreting User Needs

I think one of the most important parts of software development is understanding what the user actually needs before beginning
development. User stories help with this by describing features from the user's perspective and explaining what they hope to
accomplish. They give developers a clearer understanding of the purpose behind a feature rather than just a list of technical
requirements. In the SNHU Travel project, user stories helped demonstrate how customer needs could be translated into smaller,
manageable tasks that could be prioritized and completed during sprints.

My Approach to Software Development

When developing programs, I think it's important to break larger problems into smaller tasks and approach them one step at a
time. One thing I've learned from this course is that software development doesn't always follow a predictable path, and
requirements can change as a project progresses. In my future development work, I would like to incorporate Agile practices
such as sprint planning, regular communication, and retrospectives. I especially like the idea of developing software
incrementally and using feedback throughout the process rather than waiting until the end to discover problems.

Being a Good Team Member

I believe being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, being dependable, and being willing to
work with others to solve problems. Developers need to understand their own responsibilities while also recognizing how their
work affects the rest of the team. It's also important to be open to feedback and willing to adapt when something isn't
working. Throughout this course, I've learned that successful software development depends on more than technical ability. A
team that communicates effectively and works toward shared goals is more likely to produce a quality product.
